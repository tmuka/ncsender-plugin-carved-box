        Getting started

        1. Not sure where to begin? Guided Setup in the Start card asks six questions about the box and your machine, sets it up so it fits together, and lists the programs to run and how to put it together.
        2. For a quick start, pick a preset in the Start card: it loads right away and sets the box's shape, size and options. Save your own settings there under a name, to load again later.
        3. Measure your board and type its Stock Thickness (or tap Stock beside it, with Spoilboard Toolbox). The Height is the box closed, and the Lid Height its share, from the top to the seam where the halves meet; the base is the rest, and the lip stands above the seam, cut from the same board. Empty, each is as high as its board allows. Lower, a half's area is faced down first, and its outline still goes through the stock.
        4. Measure: Outside, the sizes and Height are the box's outside; Inside, what it holds. A round, rectangular or polygon box's sizes are then its inside (the outside is two walls more, and grows when Auto Sizes thickens the wall), and the Height its inside, from the floor to the underside of the top. An oval's, heart's or drawing's size stays its outside. Switching it shows the same box the other way.
        5. Pick the Shape and type its outside size.
        6. In Tool & Feeds, pick the bit from the Tool Library and the Material. The feeds fill in for them.
        7. Turn on Auto Sizes: the wall, lip, clearance, tabs and the rest fill in (in color) to fit each other. Type in one to set it yourself.
        8. Cut a Test Fit in scrap of the same board, and try the lid on (below).
        9. Press Generate, then in ncSender zero X and Y where Layout puts the origin (the stock's front-left corner, or the middle of the pair), and Z on the top of the stock.
        10. Hold the board down outside the cuts (the Edge Margin keeps the bit away from the edges) and run it. Every move sideways is up at ncSender's Safe Z, over your clamps.
        11. Afterwards, cut the tabs and sand them flush. The lid goes on turned over: its top is the board's underside.

        Your own shape

        Pick Drawing and open an SVG or DXF file (from Inkscape, Illustrator, Affinity, Fusion, a CAD program, or a free shape online). Its biggest closed shape is the box's outline, seen from above with the top of the drawing to the back; text, open lines and smaller shapes are left out. In a DXF, lines and arcs that meet end to end make one shape; shapes inside blocks aren't read (explode them first). The Width and Length stretch it to size, starting at the file's own size when it says one (in mm or inches); with Keep its proportions on, typing one sets the other.

        The Corner Radius rounds the drawing's outside corners, as the lip needs (Auto Sizes sets it); its inside corners stay as drawn, and a bit can't cut them sharper than its own radius, so the outline's are rounded that much. The lid is cut as the drawing's mirror image, so it lands on the base when it's turned over. An outline that crosses itself can't be cut; nor can a neck narrower than the Corner Radius rounds away. The drawing is saved with your settings.

        How the lid fits

        One half has a lip: the inner part of its wall, left standing. The other half's opening is widened by the Lip Thickness plus the Clearance, as deep as the Lip Height; the lip stands up the Clearance less, so it slides in, stops short of the bottom, and the halves close tight on the shoulder outside it. When the opening would reach the inside of its half, or come within 1 mm of it, the Lip Height is made as deep as that inside, so the two are one flat pocket with no step, and a note says so.         How the lid goes on, and what holds it:

        • Straight lip (as it comes): the lip and the opening have straight walls, so the lid grips by friction. How tight is the Clearance: try it with Test Fit.
        • Lead-In (Edges, a V-bit or roundover bit): the lip's top outer edge and the opening's edge chamfered or rounded, so the lid finds its way on. It still grips on the straight walls below.
        • Sloped Lip (Edges, a V-bit): the lip's whole side and the opening's wall sloped at the V-bit's angle. The lid drops on and centers itself, and lifts straight off: nothing grips it, so add Magnets to hold it shut. The V-bit has to be wide enough for the slope: a 90° one about twice the Lip Height across.
        • Sloped Lip with a Slope Height: only the lip's top that far is sloped; below it the lip is straight and grips at the Clearance. The opening narrows to match at its floor, in small steps of the box's bit, out of sight inside the closed box. The lid centers itself as it goes on, then grips. It needs a smaller V-bit than a whole slope.
        • Magnets hold the lid shut with any of these: the only hold for a whole sloped lip, or a looser Clearance for a lid that won't stick when the wood swells.

        Test Fit

        Test Fit makes a program for a small box with the same fit as yours: the same wall, lip, Clearance, bit and feeds, but only a little wider than its wall and only as deep inside as the lip needs. It takes a few minutes and a scrap of the same board. Cut it, and try the lid on:

        • Too tight (you have to force it): make the Clearance a little bigger, by about 0.05 mm (0.002").
        • Too loose (it rattles or falls off): make it a little smaller.
        • Wood moves with the weather: a lid that's just snug in a dry shop can stick later. About 0.2–0.3 mm suits most wood, 0.1 mm plastic.

        A rectangle's test is square and a polygon's has the same sides, with the same corner radius; an oval's is round; a heart's is a small heart; a drawing's is a small copy, in its proportions. Magnets, the notch, edges and floor corners are left out. With your settings:

        The lid on its own board, and grain matching

        In Layout, Cut Base Only or Lid Only makes a program for one half on its own board; run the other with the other button. The lid's board can be thinner than the base's: type its Lid Board thickness. Each half is zeroed on top of its own board.

        Grain Match carves the base and the lid from the two leaves of a board you've resawn, so the grain runs on across the seam when the box is closed:

        1. Resaw a board into two leaves. Plane or sand the sawn faces flat; they become the faces where the halves meet.
        2. Lay the base's leaf sawn face up and cut the Base Only, zeroed at its front-left corner.
        3. Open the other leaf like a book (turned over side to side; with the halves along Y, front to back), sawn face up, and cut the Lid Only, zeroed at the corner that was over the base's front-left: now its front-right (along Y, its back-left). With the origin in the middle, zero both in the middle.
        4. Turn the lid over onto the base the same way (side to side; along Y, front to back): it lands where its wood was, and the grain lines up.

        Nested sets

        Turn on Nested Set for 2 to 5 boxes, each fitting inside the one before with the Nest Gap all round it and over it. Each is smaller by the wall and the gap (a heart or oval a little more, to fit everywhere), and lower to fit under the lid; with Auto Sizes on, each gets its own sizes.

        • The whole set: one program, all the boxes on one board. The smaller boxes' halves are faced down to their heights first, which takes a while.
        • Box 2 on its own (and so on): one box, from a board as thick as it needs, so there's little to face away. The Nested Set card lists each box's size and board.

        A glued-on floor, for big boxes

        Most of a big box's time goes into pocketing out its inside. Turn on Glued-On Floor and the base becomes a ring: its inside is cut out through, round the wall, instead of pocketed, and the ring is glued onto a floor. The lid is cut as before.

        • The ring's inside is cut through like its outline, with tabs holding the middle, after the ring's other cuts. The middle comes out whole: keep it for something smaller. Its wall gets a finishing lap like the others, lifting over the tabs.
        • The floor is cut round its outline, its edge cut down by the Locating Step, leaving a plug the Clearance smaller than the ring's inside. The ring drops onto the step, over the plug, and lines up by itself while the glue sets. (On a heart, a drawing or a narrow oval, the plug's corners are rounded where the bit rounds the ring's, so it still fits snugly along the walls.)
        • Floor Board empty: the floor is cut from the stock, beside the ring and the lid. A thinner board, such as 1/4" plywood: cut the ring and lid as usual, then the floor with Cut: Floor Only on that board.
        • The inside of the box starts at the plug's top; the closed height counts the floor less its step. There's no Base Floor, and no Floor Corners in the ring (the lid's still get them).
        • Oversize cuts the floor that much bigger all round (1 to 2 mm), its step out to the edge. Then the ring needn't sit exactly on the floor's edge, and squeeze-out goes onto what's trimmed off: once the glue's set, run a flush-trim bit round it, its bearing on the ring.
        • Glue the ring on the step, clamp it flat, and wipe off what squeezes out inside.

        An inset floor, out of sight

        Set Floor to Inset, from below and no joint shows from the outside. The ring's inside is the lid's opening, cut out through, and a rabbet is cut round it on the face that's up while it's cut. Turn the ring over: the rabbet is underneath, and a flat panel drops into it from below.

        • The panel is the rabbet's shape, the Clearance smaller. Cut it from the Floor Board (1/4" plywood, or a contrasting wood) with Cut: Floor Only.
        • Rabbet Width is how far it reaches into the wall; the ring keeps at least 2 mm outside it. Recess sits the panel a little up inside it, so the box stands on the ring and doesn't rock if the panel bows.
        • The ring's top is the board's underside while it's cut, so nothing is cut there: the lip goes on the lid, into the ring's opening. Magnets, finger notches and a sloped lip aren't for it, and the ring's edges are left as they are.
        • Glue the panel in with the ring still rabbet side up, as it was cut, the panel the way up it was cut too; then turn the base over. (A drawing's ring and lid are both cut from the mirrored drawing, so they land as drawn.)
        • Glue a plywood panel all round. A solid-wood panel moves across its grain: glue it only at the middle of the front and back, so it can't split the ring.

        A drop-in lid

        The mirror of the inset floor, at the top. With a Stepped Glued-On Floor, set Lid to Drop-In: a rabbet is cut round the ring's top as deep as the Floor Board, and the lid is a flat panel from that board, cut beside the floor with Cut: Floor and Lid. It drops in flush and lifts out. Lip Thickness is how far the panel laps onto the ledge; Clearance is the gap round it.

        • The ring is cut from its top only, so the top and the floor are opposites: a drop-in lid takes a stepped floor (glued on underneath); an inset floor (its rabbet underneath) takes a lid with a lip.
        • A Finger Notch is a scoop at the rabbet's edge, beside and under the panel, to lift it by. Magnets and a sloped lip aren't for it; edges are cut on the ring only.
        • Plywood stays flat. A solid-wood panel moves across its grain: give it more Clearance across the grain.

        A solid lid

        Set Lid to Solid and the lid isn't hollowed out: its lip is left solid, a plug under a flat top that fits the base's opening. It's quicker, and it can be cut from a thinner board with Cut: Lid Only. The lip goes on the lid. With an inset floor, both are flat panels from one setup.

        Compartments

        Turn on Compartments to split the base's inside with dividers. They're left standing as the inside is carved out, in the same wood as the walls, so there's nothing to glue.

        • Grid: Rows front to back and Columns left to right. A rectangle's compartments are rounded rectangles; in other shapes, the grid is cut off by the walls.
        • Wedges: Sections out from the middle, like slices. Angle turns the first divider from the back, counterclockwise seen from above. Middle adds a round compartment in the middle, that wide across.
        • Uneven sizes: type each one's share. Column Sizes of 1 2 1 makes the middle column twice as wide as the other two; Row Sizes of 2 1 makes the front row twice as deep as the back. Section Sizes go round counterclockwise from the first divider: 1 2 1 makes a half and two quarters, and 3 1 makes three quarters and one quarter. Typing them sets the Rows, Columns or Sections to how many numbers you typed. Empty: all the same.
        • Divider Thickness: 1/4" (6 mm) is sturdy in wood; under 2.5 mm can split along the grain. Divider Height is from the floor. Empty is full height, just under the lid's lip; lower makes small compartments easier to reach into.
        • The note under the card gives each compartment's size, or each section's angle. Each one has to be wider than the bit; if one isn't, a problem says so.
        • With Floor Corners on, each compartment's floor is rounded. With an Edges Inside Rim, each compartment's top edge is chamfered or rounded along the dividers, stopping short where the edge bit would hit the wall.
        • Not with a Glued-On Floor or a Drop-In lid (the ring is cut through, so the dividers would come loose), or a Nested Set (the smaller boxes fill the inside).

        Lid Tray

        Turn on Lid Tray for a dish in the lid's top, so the closed box works as a tray for keys, rings or coins. The lid's top is the board's underside while the box is carved, so the dish is carved first, with the board turned over. Two dowel pins put the board back in exactly the right place for the box.

        1. Put the board on the table underside up, zero X and Y on its front-left corner and Z on its top, and run Cut: Tray (Turned Over). It bores two pin holes through the board and 3 mm into the spoilboard, on the line between the halves, then carves the dish where the lid will be.
        2. Turn the board over end for end, across that line, the way the lid turns over onto the base. Drop a dowel pin through each hole into the spoilboard.
        3. Don't zero X and Y again. Zero Z on the new top, and run Cut: Both, Side by Side. The box is carved and cut out as usual, the lid right over the dish.
        • Tray Depth: how deep the dish is. The Lid Top must be at least 3 mm (1/8") more, for the wood under the dish; Auto Sizes makes it thicker when the lid has room.
        • Tray Rim: the flat rim round the dish, in from the lid's edge. Empty is as wide as the Wall.
        • Pin Diameter: the pins, such as 1/4" steel dowel pins. The holes are bored with the box's bit, so it must be no wider than the pins; a 1/4" bit for 1/4" pins plunges straight down for a snug fit.
        • Tray Edges (with Edges on): the Edge Bit chamfers or rounds the lid's top edge and the dish's edge, after a tool change. Without it, the closed box's top edge is left square.
        • Rounded Floor (with Floor Corners on): the dish's floor corners rounded with the Corner Bit.
        • The board is a little bigger, for the pins past the parts. The top view shows them, and with Tray (Turned Over) it shows the board as it lies turned over.
        • Not with a Drop-In lid, Grain Match or a lid on its own board, an Inset floor, or a Nested Set.

        Auto Sizes

        Fills in the sizes that have to fit each other for your box's size, stock, bit and material: a wall about 8% of the size, the lip half the wall (with magnets as thin as leaves their holes room, down to 1/4") and (in wood) at least 1/4" high, the Clearance from the material, the lid as high as the stock, holding tabs, and the magnets, notch and edges when they're on. It makes the wall thicker until they fit. The sizes it fills in are in color; typing in one sets it yourself and turns Auto Sizes off.

        Other bits: floor corners and edges

        Magnets: with a tool changer set up in ncSender, pick a smaller Magnet Bit (an end mill, not a drill) from the Tool Library: the program changes to it for the holes after the outlines, with starting feeds for its size in your material. A 1/4" box bit can't cut 5 mm holes, a 1/8" one can.

        Floor Corners rounds where each pocket's floor meets its wall, with a ball or bowl bit. Edges chamfers or rounds the edges with a V-bit or roundover bit: the Lead-In on the lip's outer edge and the opening's edge helps the lid find its way on. With a V-bit, Sloped Lip slopes the lip instead (see How the lid fits): all of it, or only its top as far as the Slope Height. Both are cut after the outlines, while the tabs still hold the halves.

        • With a tool changer set up in ncSender (a manual one too) and the bits picked from the Tool Library, Generate makes one program that changes bits (M6).
        • Otherwise, cut the box, put the other bit in, zero Z on the top of the stock (keep X and Y), and run Corners Only or Edges Only.

        Tips

        • A finishing pass always goes round the walls once more after the roughing, for cleaner walls; how much it takes and which way it goes are set by the Material.
        • Enlarge a picture: click a blank spot on the top or side view (or ⤢ by its title) to see it over the whole window. There the mouse wheel zooms at the pointer, two fingers pinch, and zoomed in a drag moves it; or use −, + and Fit at the top. Click a blank spot, ×, or press Esc to go back. A tap on a label still goes to its setting.
        • On a slower computer, such as a Raspberry Pi, the views wait until you stop typing before they update, so the keys keep up. A heart, an oval or a drawing always waits.
        • A tight lid: two Finger Notches opposite each other (front and back, or a Start Angle of 90 for the sides) lift it straight up with both hands. From one it tips and binds.
        • Keep the Holding Tabs on: they hold the halves while the outlines, corners and edges are cut.
        • Tap a label on the pictures (Wall, Lip Height, Clearance, Gap…) to go to its field; tap a problem under the settings to go to the field it's about.
        • Field values take either unit and simple math: 1/4", 6.35mm, 1-1/2 in, 1in+4mm.
