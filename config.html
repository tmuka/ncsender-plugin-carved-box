Getting started

  1. For a quick start, pick a preset in the Start card: it loads right away and sets the box's shape, size and options. Save your own settings there under a name, to load again later.
  2. Measure your board and type its Stock Thickness (or tap Stock beside it, with Spoilboard Toolbox). The base is the full thickness; so is the lid, unless you lower its Lid Height.
  3. Pick the Shape and type its outside size.
  4. In Tool & Feeds, pick the bit from the Tool Library and the Material. The feeds fill in for them.
  5. Turn on Auto Sizes: the wall, lip, clearance, tabs and the rest fill in (in color) to fit each other. Type in one to set it yourself.
  6. Cut a Test Fit in scrap of the same board, and try the lid on (below).
  7. Press Generate, then in ncSender zero X and Y where Layout puts the origin (the stock's front-left corner, or the middle of the pair), and Z on the top of the stock.
  8. Hold the board down outside the cuts (the Edge Margin keeps the bit away from the edges) and run it. Every move sideways is up at ncSender's Safe Z, over your clamps.
  9. Afterwards, cut the tabs and sand them flush. The lid goes on turned over: its top is the board's underside.

How the lid fits

One half has a lip: the inner part of its wall, left standing. The other half's opening is widened by the Lip Thickness plus the Clearance, as deep as the lip plus the Clearance, so the lip slides in and the halves meet on the shoulder outside it. Your box, closed and cut through its middle:

Test Fit

Test Fit makes a program for a small box with the same fit as yours: the same wall, lip, Clearance, bit and feeds, but only a little wider than its wall and only as deep inside as the lip needs. It takes a few minutes and a scrap of the same board. Cut it, and try the lid on:

  • Too tight (you have to force it): make the Clearance a little bigger, by about 0.05 mm (0.002").
  • Too loose (it rattles or falls off): make it a little smaller.
  • Wood moves with the weather: a lid that's just snug in a dry shop can stick later. About 0.2–0.3 mm suits most wood, 0.1 mm plastic.

A rectangle's test is square and a polygon's has the same sides, with the same corner radius; an oval's is round; a heart's is a small heart. Magnets, the notch, edges and floor corners are left out. With your settings:

The lid on its own board, and grain matching

In Layout, Cut Base Only or Lid Only makes a program for one half on its own board; run the other with the other button. The lid's board can be thinner than the base's: type its Lid Board thickness. Each half is zeroed on top of its own board.

Grain Match carves the base and the lid from the two leaves of a board you've resawn, so the grain runs on across the seam when the box is closed:

  1. Resaw a board into two leaves. Plane or sand the sawn faces flat; they become the faces where the halves meet.
  2. Lay the base's leaf sawn face up and cut the Base Only, zeroed at its front-left corner.
  3. Open the other leaf like a book (turned over side to side; with the halves along Y, front to back), sawn face up, and cut the Lid Only, zeroed at the corner that was over the base's front-left: now its front-right (along Y, its back-left). With the origin in the middle, zero both in the middle.
  4. Turn the lid over onto the base the same way (side to side; along Y, front to back): it lands where its wood was, and the grain lines up.

Nested sets

Turn on Nested Set for 2 to 5 boxes, each fitting inside the one before with the Nest Gap all round it and over it. Each is smaller by the wall and the gap (a heart or oval a little more, to fit everywhere), and lower to fit under the lid; with Auto Sizes on, each gets its own sizes.

  • The whole set: one program, all the boxes on one board. The smaller boxes' halves are faced down to their heights first, which takes a while.
  • Box 2 on its own (and so on): one box, from a board as thick as it needs, so there's little to face away. The Nested Set card lists each box's size and board.

Auto Sizes

Fills in the sizes that have to fit each other for your box's size, stock, bit and material: a wall about 8% of the size, the lip half the wall (with magnets as thin as leaves their holes room, down to 1/4") and (in wood) at least 1/4" high, the Clearance from the material, the lid as high as the stock, holding tabs, and the magnets, notch and edges when they're on. It makes the wall thicker until they fit. The sizes it fills in are in color; typing in one sets it yourself and turns Auto Sizes off.

Other bits: floor corners and edges

Magnets: with a tool changer set up in ncSender, pick a smaller Magnet Bit (an end mill, not a drill) from the Tool Library: the program changes to it for the holes after the outlines, with starting feeds for its size in your material. A 1/4" box bit can't cut 5 mm holes, a 1/8" one can.

Floor Corners rounds where each pocket's floor meets its wall, with a ball or bowl bit. Edges chamfers or rounds the edges with a V-bit or roundover bit: the Lead-In on the lip's outer edge and the opening's edge helps the lid find its way on. Both are cut after the outlines, while the tabs still hold the halves.

  • With a tool changer set up in ncSender (a manual one too) and the bits picked from the Tool Library, Generate makes one program that changes bits (M6).
  • Otherwise, cut the box, put the other bit in, zero Z on the top of the stock (keep X and Y), and run Corners Only or Edges Only.

Tips

  • The Finishing Pass goes round the walls once more after the roughing, for cleaner walls; how much it takes and which way it goes are set by the Material.
  • Keep the Holding Tabs on: they hold the halves while the outlines, corners and edges are cut.
  • Tap a label on the pictures (Wall, Lip Height, Clearance, Gap…) to go to its field; tap a problem under the settings to go to the field it's about.
  • Field values take either unit and simple math: 1/4", 6.35mm, 1-1/2 in, 1in+4mm.
